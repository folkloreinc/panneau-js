# 0059: Flags of regions

- Status: proposed
- Date: 2026-11-04
- Changes: the `region` type of [0047](0047-countries-regions-and-phone-numbers.md)

## Context

[0047](0047-countries-regions-and-phone-numbers.md) shows countries with their flags, as emoji: two regional indicator symbols, which every system font draws. Regions have no flag. In an address form, the country has its flag and the province or the state below it doesn't, and in a list of orders, a column of provinces is a column of names.

Emoji fonts don't draw the flags of regions. Unicode has tag sequences for them, but fonts only draw those of England, Scotland and Wales. So flags of regions are images, and they need a source:

- **`flag-icons`** and **`country-flag-icons`** (MIT, maintained) have the flags of countries, and none of the states or provinces.
- **`circle-flags`** (MIT) has round flags, of 33 American states and territories and of 2 provinces.
- **`region-flags`** (public domain, from Wikimedia Commons) has the 13 provinces and territories of Canada and the 50 states and the district of the United States. Its files are the SVGs of Commons, 10.8 MB for these 64 flags, and haven't been updated since 2017: Mississippi, Minnesota and Utah have new flags since.
- **A CDN of flags** would load them from a third party, from each admin.

## Decision

- **Source:** the SVGs of the flags on Wikimedia Commons, which are the current flags. `scripts/region-flags.js` lists the file of each flag, downloads it, and checks its license on Commons: public domain, CC0 or "copyrighted free use", or the script fails. Mississippi's flag is "copyrighted free use", the others are in the public domain.
- **Format:** each flag is rendered in Chromium at 32 pixels high, twice its size in a field, and kept as a WebP data URL, without the color profile that Chromium adds: under 1 KB a flag. Each country is a module of `@panneau/fields/region`, generated by the script, with the page and the license of each flag on Commons: `flags/CA.ts` (13 flags, 14 KB) and `flags/US.ts` (the 50 states, the district and the 6 territories, 45 KB).
- **Loading:** a country's module loads with `import()`, the first time a region of that country shows, in a field or a list. A form or a list without a region of Canada or the United States doesn't load any flag. Until its module loads, and for a region without a flag, a region shows without one.
- **Display:** an `<img>` with an empty `alt`, since the name of the region is next to it, in a box the size of a flag emoji (1.25em by 0.9375em), the flag contained in it whatever its shape. Fields show it before the selected region and in the options, and lists before the name of the region.
- **Other countries:** their regions have no flags. Adding a country is adding its files to the script, running it, and adding its module to the loaders of `flags.ts`.
- **API:** `@panneau/fields/region` exports `RegionFlag` (the image of a region's flag, or nothing), `useRegionFlags()`, `loadRegionFlags()`, `getRegionFlag()` and `REGION_FLAG_COUNTRIES`.

## Consequences

- `@panneau/fields` gets no dependency, and its build has one more module per country, outside the bundles of the forms that have no region of that country.
- The flags are data URLs: a Content Security Policy needs `img-src data:`, which the policy of `@panneau/ui`'s README already has.
- Flags change rarely; when one does, running the script updates it.
- `laravel-panneau` doesn't change: the value of a region is still its code.
